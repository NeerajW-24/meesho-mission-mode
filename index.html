Why it scales to 27.4 crore buyers

  • Clear Listings runs offline, once per listing. The clean title is stored as a field, so serving it costs nothing extra whether 1 lakh or 27 crore people open it. Cost grows with listings, not users.
  • It reuses Meesho's own capability. Meesho's image-tagging models (2022) matched or beat human taggers on kurtis and cut ~60% of tagging work across 3,000+ categories. Walmart and Amazon already use LLMs on listings.
  • Pack Proof is risk-based. One photo, only on orders ≥ ₹499, high-claim categories and new sellers. Estimated ₹0.30 per check in the model.
  • Safety rails: only facts from fields and photos, a confidence threshold, a "flag this title" button for sellers, human QC on top SKUs, and an A/B test before rollout.

How Raju earns, and why he will bother

  • Out of stock no longer means a lost customer. He scans the buyer in, the order ships from any Meesho seller, and he earns on it.
  • Referral window: 90 days. Every order that buyer places for 90 days pays Raju 1.5%.
  • Phase 2: list his own stock on Meesho and ship locally via Valmo. This is where real money is: full product margin, not a commission.
  • Trust point for his category: customers drop returns for phone and accessory orders at his counter and ask him for help. Shoes and clothes still go back by home pickup. Every visit brings people back into his shop.
  • Commission covers every category a referred buyer orders for 90 days, because Raju brought that customer to Meesho.
  • Honest constraint: at 1.5% alone a shop earns only ~₹1,000–2,000 a year, so the ₹50 first-order bonus and phase 2 matter. Both are in the model.
